# Constitution of Schemata

**Draft v0.1.1**

## Preamble

Recognizing that people are not angels, that worthwhile work often fails to begin or continue, and that no institution can command virtue;

Believing that learning grows through accumulation and transformation—through gathering, making, testing, correcting, preserving, and beginning again;

Learning through what we create, creating so that others may learn, preserving what might otherwise disappear, promoting work before it is abandoned, catalyzing change when possibility appears, and examining change before it becomes error;

Seeking balance between content and form, freedom and restraint, possibility and feasibility, trust and verification, continuity and renewal;

Holding that participation should remain voluntary, that contribution should receive lasting and accurate credit, and that knowledge, process, and useful tools should be made openly available, subject to law, safety, privacy, licenses, and practical limits;

Knowing that Government is itself made by imperfect people, and therefore must be strong enough to preserve work, limited enough to protect choice and Project autonomy, and simple enough to be understood and used;

We establish this Constitution of Schemata.

Schemata begins in learning, takes form through creation, and returns its work to learning. Its Government shall serve its Projects; its Projects shall serve learning; and what we build should not end with us.

## Article I — Foundations

### Section 1. Identity and Character

1. The name of this association is **Schemata**.

2. Schemata is a student-founded, project-centered, voluntary association based at Nichols School and open to broader participation as this Constitution permits.

3. Schemata consists of a Central Government and its Recognized Projects, joined in a federal system under this Constitution.

4. Schemata may operate through a school club or another institutionally recognized form. That form provides a lawful basis for activity within the institution, but neither defines the whole of Schemata nor replaces its constitutional Government.

### Section 2. Purpose and Work

1. The ultimate purpose of Schemata is to advance learning.

2. Schemata advances learning by helping useful work begin, develop, survive, and remain available. It may initiate, support, recognize, connect, preserve, and publish Projects and Initiatives.

3. Its work should create public value by organizing, applying, communicating, or giving form to knowledge. Within lawful and practical limits, both process and result should be publicly accessible, openly licensed where applicable, maintainable, and free to use.

4. Schemata is not operated for private profit. Resources may be accepted or used only for its purposes and under applicable institutional rules; no Member or Office is obliged to provide them.

5. Responsible failures, unfinished work, and abandoned approaches may be preserved when they can contribute to future learning.

### Section 3. Constitutional Order

1. The Central Government preserves Schemata's common purpose, federal relationships, institutional memory, and conditions for continuity. It has no end independent of learning and the Projects it serves.

2. A Recognized Project retains its distinct identity, purpose, Government, and reserved authority. Recognition creates a constitutional relationship; it does not create ownership.

3. Participation remains voluntary. No Government established by this Constitution possesses political sovereignty or coercive authority over persons. In this Constitution, **Government**, **federalism**, **sovereignty**, and related terms describe limited organizational relationships only.

4. This Constitution establishes Schemata's durable framework. Governing Documents adopted under it may implement and revise ordinary arrangements without acquiring constitutional rank.

### Section 4. Applicable Rules and Relations

1. Conduct under Schemata remains subject, within proper scope, to applicable law and regulation; Nichols School policies; platform rules; licenses and agreements; and identifiable standards of academic integrity, attribution, privacy, safety, and responsible use.

2. An external rule applies only within the jurisdiction or relationship from which it arises. Recognition, advice, funding, hosting, or technical support creates no authority beyond what has been lawfully accepted.

3. Schemata shall not represent itself as speaking for another institution, nor bind Schemata, a Project, another person, or an external body to an obligation, without authority.

4. This Constitution creates an internal organizational order. It claims no legal status and promises no resources or performance beyond those lawfully obtained and reasonably available.

## Article II — Projects and Recognition

### Section 1. Initiatives and Projects

1. An **Initiative** is a proposal, experiment, prototype, limited undertaking, or developing body of work that has not become a Project.

2. Schemata neither licenses ideas nor requires formal acceptance before an Initiative begins. When an Initiative is consistent with learning, public value, this Constitution, and applicable rules, Schemata should support it within available capacity. Use of Schemata's name, resources, systems, or institutional access nevertheless requires authorization.

3. A **Project** is a sufficiently developed undertaking with an independent purpose, a scope capable of continued work, a Government, and durable Records. It should serve learning or apply knowledge for public benefit and should maintain an accessible online form or archive where reasonably practicable.

4. A work may remain an Initiative, join an existing Project, or grow into a Project of its own. No discipline, technology, or size alone determines the result.

### Section 2. Recognition

1. Only a Project may become a **Recognized Project** of Schemata.

2. Recognition requires mutual acceptance by the Central Government and the competent Project authority. During lawful formation or Reconstruction, that authority may be the Project's Founder or Reconstructor.

3. Recognition shall be expressed in a Project Constitution and Recognition Record identifying the Project, its Founders or Reconstructors, its Government, the basis of the relationship, and any material variation in Central involvement.

4. Recognition affirms a relationship, not ownership, guaranteed success, or blanket endorsement of every statement or act of the Project.

5. A Project remains responsible for the obligations, expenses, donations, and resources it accepts unless the Central Government expressly accepts responsibility under applicable rules.

### Section 3. Project Autonomy and Federal Authority

1. A Recognized Project governs its purpose, content, methods, internal organization, and development through its Project Constitution.

2. The Central Government may act toward a Project only under this Constitution and authority accepted through the Recognition relationship. Central Policy alone cannot create new authority over a Project.

3. The degree of Central assistance, supervision, custody, or intervention may differ among Projects, but a material difference must be stated in the Project Constitution or Recognition Record rather than presumed.

4. A Project may alter matters wholly internal to itself. A change affecting Central authority, Central duties, Recognition, archives, or Reconstruction requires mutual acceptance and an updated Record.

### Section 4. Change and End of Recognition

1. The Central Government and a Recognized Project may each propose or refuse a change, suspend performance where authorized, or end Recognition under this Constitution.

2. No Project may be compelled to remain in Schemata, and Schemata may not be compelled to continue active Recognition.

3. Ending Recognition ends the federal relationship prospectively. It does not erase the Project's prior status, Contributions, Credit, Records, licenses, or true history.

4. If the parties cannot reconcile their governing instruments, either may end Recognition; neither may rewrite the shared relationship alone.

### Section 5. Archives and Contribution Records

1. Recognition should establish an appropriate archival arrangement. Authority to preserve may arise from a license, permission, mirror or deposit, custody, or another lawful arrangement.

2. Archival authority confers neither ownership nor operational control and should extend no further than reasonably necessary for agreed preservation and continuity.

3. Each Recognized Project shall maintain Contribution Records appropriate to its work, acknowledge its Contributors under Article III, and preserve both through changes of status to the greatest lawful and reasonably practicable extent.

## Article III — Formal Membership

### Section 1. Eligibility and Assent

1. Formal Membership is open to every current Nichols Upper School student.

2. Nichols Middle School students, faculty, staff, alumni, and persons outside Nichols may also become Formal Members where participation is lawful, appropriate, and consistent with Schemata's purposes.

3. External participation may be limited for safety, supervision, privacy, data protection, institutional access, or protection of minors. It shall not be used primarily for commercial promotion, private exploitation, unauthorized research, or personal-data collection.

4. A person becomes a **Formal Member** through recorded voluntary assent to this Constitution and the Governing Documents applicable to Membership.

5. Formal Membership is distinct from school-club membership or another externally created status. No experience, promised work, Project participation, payment, or donation is required.

### Section 2. Standing and Freedom

1. Formal Members may receive information, propose action, participate in deliberation, join Projects under their governing instruments, and accept responsibilities for which they are eligible.

2. Members participate in governance through proposals, discussion, consultation, objection, and review. Formal authority to adopt decisions is entrusted to Offices so that responsibility remains identifiable.

3. Membership does not itself grant an Office, privileged access, custody, authorship, ownership, or authority over a Project.

4. Attendance, contribution, Project service, and continued participation are voluntary except for a duty specifically and knowingly accepted.

### Section 3. Contributions and Credit

1. A bona fide **Contribution** to Schemata or a Project should be recorded in a manner appropriate to its nature and the applicable Governing Documents.

2. Every Contributor is entitled to accurate **Credit**. Subject to law, privacy, licenses, release timing, and shared-work integrity, a Contributor may receive acknowledgment, describe the work, use a preferred name or pseudonym, or choose public anonymity.

3. Public anonymity permits a limited internal identity Record where required for safety, accountability, institutional rules, or a genuine dispute.

4. Credit records fact; it does not rank persons. Later correction, replacement, or discontinuation of work does not erase a genuine Contribution. Attribution may be corrected when materially false, including through fraud, plagiarism, false identification, or lack of the claimed Contribution.

5. Formal Membership is not a condition of Contribution or Credit.

### Section 4. Responsibilities and Entrusted Authority

1. Formal Members shall respect this Constitution, applicable rules and Governing Documents, licenses, privacy, safety, academic integrity, and others' lawful rights.

2. Specific duties arise from an accepted Office, assignment, delegation, access privilege, custody, agreement, or other voluntary undertaking—not from Membership alone.

3. A person entrusted with Records, systems, credentials, funds, or materials shall use them only within the authority granted and protect and return them as required.

4. Every Constitutional Officeholder shall be an eligible Formal Member unless this Constitution expressly provides otherwise.

### Section 5. Transition and End of Membership

1. A Member may withdraw at any time, subject only to reasonable handoff of entrusted access, Records, or property.

2. The end of Membership does not erase prior Contributions, Credit, authorship, ownership, licenses, Office history, or true Records.

3. Where a foreseeable change in affiliation or eligibility may affect Membership, Schemata should, when reasonably practicable, consult the Member and respect continuing assent, applicable eligibility, and the Member's choice.

4. Office, Project participation, access, and Membership are distinct statuses. Adverse restriction or termination requires applicable authority, stated reasons, and, where circumstances permit, a reasonable opportunity to respond. Procedures belong in Governing Documents.

### Section 6. Participation Without Formal Membership

1. Non-Members may in principle follow Schemata, receive updates, use public work, offer ideas, test tools, and participate informally as applicable Governing Documents permit.

2. Informal participation grants neither Constitutional Office nor privileged write access; special access must be expressly authorized and supervised.

3. A non-Member's genuine Contribution receives the protections of Section 3. Terms such as **Visitor** may be used by Policy or ordinary communication but create no additional constitutional class of Membership.

## Article IV — Governments and Offices

### Section 1. Nature and Powers of Government

1. A **Government** is the internal structure of Offices through which Schemata or a Recognized Project exercises authority and accepts responsibility. Schemata has a Central Government; each Recognized Project has a Project Government.

2. The Central Government possesses only authority granted by this Constitution or accepted through a Recognition relationship, together with measures reasonably necessary and proper to carry that authority into effect.

3. Necessary and proper measures shall not create a new substantive power, evade a limitation, or invade authority reserved to another Government, Office, or person.

4. Powers not granted to the Central Government, nor prohibited to a Project, remain with the respective Project. Choices not entrusted to a Government remain with the person or body to whom they properly belong.

### Section 2. Offices and Officeholders

1. An **Office** is a continuing allocation of authority and responsibility. An **Officeholder** is the one person presently accountable for exercising it.

2. Authority belongs to the Office; responsibility rests with its Officeholder. The Office continues beyond any particular person.

3. Authority arises from the Office and its governing instruments, not from Foundership, reputation, technical access, possession, institutional title, or practical ability.

4. An Office's stated domain identifies its primary responsibility, not exclusive ownership of every related matter. Cross-domain action is governed by Article V.

### Section 3. The Central Government

1. The Central Government maintains Schemata's common purpose, federal relationships, Membership order, institutional coordination, common systems, and continuity.

2. The **Schemata Representative** has primary responsibility for Schemata's purpose, substantive direction, and Recognition relationships.

3. The **Schemata Administrator** has primary responsibility for persons, procedure, organizational Records, and institutional coordination.

4. The **Schemata Sysadmin** has primary responsibility for common systems, custody, access, integrity, availability, archives, and technical continuity.

5. Priority within a domain creates no general supremacy over the other Offices. The Central Government has no residual authority merely because it is central.

### Section 4. Project Governments

1. Each Recognized Project shall maintain a Project Director, Project Administrator, and Project Maintainer.

2. The **Project Director** is primarily responsible for purpose, substance, and development; the **Project Administrator**, for participation, procedure, coordination, and Project Records; and the **Project Maintainer**, for systems, materials, availability, maintainability, and operational continuity.

3. A Project Constitution may refine these domains, add subordinate roles, or adopt additional titles, but shall leave each constitutional responsibility identifiable.

4. Corresponding Central and Project Offices are not automatically in a chain of command. Authority between them must arise from this Constitution or the Recognition relationship.

### Section 5. Service, Delegation, and Multiple Offices

1. Each Office has one accountable Officeholder at a time. One person may hold multiple Offices when necessary, although separation should be preferred when reasonably practicable.

2. A multiple Officeholder shall distinguish the capacity in which each act is taken. Common Officeholding does not erase an Office or a concurrence required from it.

3. An Officeholder may appoint assistants or temporary acting personnel and delegate work or limited authority within an identifiable scope. Delegation does not transfer the Office.

4. The Officeholder remains responsible for reasonable selection, supervision, correction, and handoff, but not for every independent act of another person.

5. One Office, one accountable voice, and many hands shall be the ordinary relation between responsibility and work.

### Section 6. External and Advisory Capacities

1. A role created or recognized by an external institution does not thereby become a Constitutional Office.

2. Club Leaders, Faculty Advisors, Educators, information-technology personnel, and other counterparts may advise, acknowledge, approve, or act within their own capacities and authority.

3. A person may hold both an external role and a Constitutional Office, but the capacities remain distinct. External advice creates no internal authority, and internal authority displaces no external institution's lawful jurisdiction.

## Article V — Authority, Decisions, and Governing Documents

### Section 1. Authority to Decide

1. Except where this Constitution provides otherwise, Government authority is exercised through Offices. Every Decision must be authorized by at least one competent Office, directly or through a valid prior delegation.

2. An Office may decide matters within its authority and employ reasonably necessary and proper measures, but may neither create new substantive power nor invade another Office.

3. Members shape governance through proposals, deliberation, consultation, objection, and review. Their voice gains organizational force when adopted through a competent Office.

4. When an Office cannot act, its authority passes neither automatically to Members nor to a meeting or numerical majority. Article VI governs continuity and restoration.

### Section 2. Consultation, Concurrence, and Veto

1. When an action materially affects another Office's work without exercising its authority, the responsible Office should consult it before acting.

2. When an action exercises, limits, directs, transfers, or continuingly binds another Office's authority, that Office must expressly concur before the action takes effect. Substance and effect, not title, determine the requirement; silence is not concurrence.

3. An action lacking required concurrence is ineffective from the beginning. Publication, partial execution, delay, or absence of immediate objection does not cure it.

4. An Office possessing relevant authority may veto an otherwise valid Decision, stating its authority, ground, and scope promptly after learning the Decision and ground.

5. Without new facts, effects, or newly discovered grounds, unreasonable delay ordinarily defeats a veto. Later changes of judgment should proceed through revision or repeal.

### Section 3. Forms, Proposals, and Adoption

1. A **Decision** is an authorized determination or act. A **Governing Document** establishes a continuing rule, procedure, responsibility, relationship, or exercise of authority. A **Record** preserves facts, reasons, authorization, or results but creates no authority by repetition.

2. Function and actual effect, rather than title, determine an instrument's constitutional character.

3. Any Office or Formal Member may propose a Decision or Governing Document. Others may propose or comment as Governing Documents permit.

4. A proposal has no organizational force until adopted by the competent Offices. They may accept, revise, reject, or defer it; a material revision must receive the authorization required by its resulting scope.

5. Instruments for which another Article states additional requirements remain subject to those requirements.

### Section 4. Effect, Publication, and Acknowledgment

1. A Decision takes effect only after complete authorization and any stated conditions. A continuing Governing Document must also be published and preserved in a form reasonably available to those it governs.

2. Reasonable notice should precede enforcement of a Decision specially and adversely affecting a person, unless immediate protection or higher authority requires otherwise.

3. Governing Documents should be publicly accessible. Privacy, safety, security, licenses, protected data, and applicable rules may justify a limited and recorded restriction.

4. Significant, contested, or continuing Decisions shall leave a Record of their authority, scope, and status. Technical publication or custody confers no control over substance.

5. An **Acknowledgment** records that an external or advisory person or body has received, reviewed, or recognized an instrument in a stated capacity. It is not constitutional concurrence and binds no acknowledging party beyond what is expressly accepted. Where external authorization is necessary, the instrument shall identify it.

### Section 5. Revision and Review

1. A competent Government may revise, repeal, replace, or allow the expiration of a Decision or Governing Document with the authorization required by its resulting scope.

2. A valid veto ends or suspends the remaining effect of the Decision within the vetoing Office's authority; it neither erases completed acts nor rewrites historical Records. Discovery that required concurrence never existed is a finding of original ineffectiveness, not a veto.

3. A provision conflicting with higher authority is ineffective to the extent of conflict. Superseded, withdrawn, vetoed, or ineffective materials should be marked and preserved when they retain historical or learning value.

## Article VI — Continuity

### Section 1. Records, Materials, and Custody

1. Schemata and its Recognized Projects shall, within lawful and reasonable capacity, preserve enough for their purpose, development, authority, Contributions, and results to be understood, verified, used, and, where possible, continued.

2. Preservation remains subject to privacy, security, licenses, applicable rules, cost, and practical capacity. Where important material cannot be retained, its prior existence, status, and reason for loss should be recorded where practicable.

3. Custody, credentials, hosting, or technical possession creates neither ownership nor governing authority. When an Office, Membership, delegation, or access relationship ends, entrusted materials and privileges shall be transferred, limited, or returned as appropriate.

4. Material Records or primary assets should not be irreversibly destroyed or transferred without explicit, recorded authority and, where reasonably practicable, verified preservation or recovery.

### Section 2. Transitions and Interruptions of Office

1. Succession shall follow applicable Governing Documents. In their absence, an Officeholder leaving normally may designate an eligible successor; otherwise, the other two Officeholders may designate one jointly. A successor must accept the Office.

2. A departing Officeholder should, where possible, provide notice, identify pending matters, and transfer necessary Records, custody, and access. An Office may designate limited acting authority during a temporary absence.

3. When service is interrupted or an Officeholder may no longer responsibly serve, action shall be reasoned, proportionate, and directed toward continuity rather than punishment. Temporary protection should be distinguished from permanent termination; notice, response, and possible return should be afforded where circumstances permit.

4. Detailed standards for acting service, suspension, removal, and review belong in Governing Documents. When ordinary measures cannot restore an effective Office or Government, Dormancy or Reconstruction shall be used rather than an unauthorized transfer of power.

### Section 3. Dormancy

1. **Dormancy** acknowledges that an entity is not presently operating through an effective Government while preserving its identity, Records, and possibility of return. It is not punishment, dissolution, or transfer of ownership.

2. During Dormancy, new substantive decisions and commitments ordinarily pause. Preservation, safety, required compliance, notice, and preparation for lawful restoration may continue under existing authority.

3. **Project Dormancy** is a status recognized by the Central Government within a Recognition relationship. It transfers no internal authority or lawful materials to Central Government and limits neither reactivation, Reconstruction, nor the Project's right to withdraw.

4. **Central Dormancy** exists when the Central Government cannot responsibly perform its constitutional functions. Central substantive authority then pauses, but Recognized Projects remain autonomous and may continue or withdraw. A school club or other institutional form may conduct minimum lawful activity without thereby becoming the Central Government.

### Section 4. Reconstruction

1. **Reconstruction** restores the continuous institutional identity of a Dormant Recognized Project or Dormant Central Government when ordinary succession or reactivation is inadequate.

2. Reconstruction shall not displace a functioning Government because of policy disagreement, fill an ordinary single-Office vacancy, or claim succession merely from similarity, reuse, or a common name.

3. A Reconstruction shall record its predecessor, basis and extent of continuity, materials continued, and material departures. It shall preserve accurate history, Credit, Contribution Records, and applicable licenses; Membership, Office, access, private property, and unaccepted obligations do not transfer automatically.

4. Temporary reconstruction authority extends only so far as necessary to restore effective Government and ends when that Government accepts responsibility.

5. Project Reconstruction requires a Reconstruction Record and renewed Central Recognition. Central Reconstruction proceeds under this Constitution and a Reconstruction Record and does not require new Founding Ratification.

6. A license may permit reuse or a fork; it does not itself confer institutional succession.

### Section 5. Permanent Dormancy and Refounding

1. **Permanent Dormancy** is the expressly recorded end of Schemata's active constitutional continuity and shall not be inferred from inactivity. It requires unanimous concurrence of the three Central Offices after reasonable notice to Formal Members and every active Recognized Project.

2. Upon Permanent Dormancy, the Central Government and active Recognition relationships end prospectively. Recognized Projects remain independent, while the Constitution, Records, Credit, archives, licenses, and accurate history should be preserved where practicable.

3. A later organization may be founded under the same name, subject to applicable rights. Such a **Refounding** is a new organization: it inherits neither former Membership, Offices, Projects, Recognition, access, assets, obligations, nor institutional approval automatically.

4. A refounded organization becomes Schemata only through new Ratification under Article VII. Reconstruction preserves identity; Refounding preserves possibility.

## Article VII — Supremacy, Interpretation, Amendment, and Ratification

### Section 1. Supremacy and Order of Authority

1. Applicable law and regulation, binding institutional rules, platform terms, licenses, and accepted obligations constrain Schemata within their proper scope.

2. This Constitution is the supreme internal Governing Document of Schemata and its Recognition relationships.

3. A Project Constitution governs its Project's internal order, subject to this Constitution as a condition of Recognition. A Recognition Record governs the shared Central–Project relationship. Neither silently amends the other.

4. Central and Project Governing Documents bind only within their authority. A lower provision is ineffective only to the extent of conflict with higher authority; a later date alone creates no superiority.

### Section 2. Constitutional Interpretation

1. Each Office interprets applicable provisions in the first instance within its responsibilities. Project Governments maintain the authoritative internal interpretation of their Project Constitutions; the Central Government maintains the authoritative institutional interpretation of this Constitution and determines how Schemata shall act under it.

2. The Preamble and Article I guide interpretation without independently granting power. Necessary and Proper authority shall be read with Reserved Powers, and ambiguity shall not create a new substantive field of authority.

3. A material interpretation shall be reasoned and recorded. It may clarify text but may not amend it, falsify history, or create authority the text and structure cannot reasonably bear.

4. A Project may state a different interpretation of its Recognition relationship. If the parties cannot reconcile their positions, Central Government determines Schemata's action, while the Project may refuse a change or withdraw.

5. Disagreement among Offices is governed by Article V. No interpretation gains force merely from a general vote, practical possession, or repeated assertion.

### Section 3. Amendment

1. An Amendment may be proposed by any Formal Member, Central Office, or Recognized Project.

2. Before it takes effect, Formal Members and every active Recognized Project shall receive timely notice, the proposed text and purpose, and a genuine opportunity to submit comment, objection, or an alternative.

3. The Central Offices shall consider material responses in good faith and record their judgment. Every Amendment requires all three Central Offices' express concurrence; no Policy, interpretation, emergency measure, or practice may amend this Constitution by implication.

4. An Amendment does not by itself rewrite a Project Constitution, Project-specific Recognition Record, ownership, license, or historical fact. If a Recognition relationship cannot conform, the parties may revise it or either may end it.

5. An Amendment shall be published with its authority, effective date, and prior text preserved. It should operate prospectively and provide a reasonable transition where existing relationships are materially affected.

6. Members remain free to withdraw. Dormant Projects shall be notified where practicable and must reconcile their relationship with the current Constitution before active Recognition resumes.

### Section 4. Ratification and Effect

1. This Constitution first takes effect through a written Ratification Record signed by the Founder and every initial Central Officeholder, each identifying the capacity in which assent and responsibility are accepted.

2. A person holding multiple initial Central Offices may sign once while accepting each capacity. Ratification establishes those Offices and the Formal Membership of eligible signers who assent as Members.

3. Club Leaders, Faculty Advisors, Educators, information-technology personnel, and other institutional counterparts may sign optional Acknowledgments or provide external authorization within their own capacities. Their signatures are not required for internal Ratification unless external authority makes them necessary for the intended institutional operation.

4. Foundership remains permanent historical Credit but creates no continuing Office, veto, ownership, or special authority after Ratification.

5. Schemata shall maintain an accessible authoritative text, version and Amendment history, and Ratification Record sufficient to verify the Constitution in force.

6. Central Reconstruction restores Government under the continuing Constitution without new Ratification. Refounding creates a new Schemata only through Ratification under this Section.
